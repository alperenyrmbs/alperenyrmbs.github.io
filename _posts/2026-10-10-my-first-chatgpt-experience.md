*My experience using AI to prepare for my career through GitHub, LinkedIn, and a personal website.*

I installed ChatGPT on my computer, added an extension to my browser, and started explaining what I wanted to do. In about 20 minutes, my GitHub and LinkedIn profiles were updated, my personal website was launched, and I began working on my first blog post. After the setup, I did not carry out each step myself. The tasks were handled automatically through ChatGPT.

What impressed me most was how quickly an idea became something I could see and use. I was able to bring several things I had wanted to do together around one goal: creating an online space where I could explain who I am, share my work, and record what I learn.

In this first post, I want to share my experience and explain why the tools mattered. The 20 minutes describes my own experience. It does not mean everyone will get the same results in the same amount of time.

## Why did I want to try this?

I study Data Science at the University of North Texas. As I prepare for my career, I think it is important to explain my skills and interests in a way other people can understand. A resume can introduce me briefly, but I also need space to share the thinking behind my projects, the problems I face, and what I learn.

My goal was to create better organized profiles for job applications and a website where I could publish my own posts. I was also curious: if I give AI a goal, how much can it do to help me reach it?

I treated the process as a small personal experiment. I wanted to make AI a practical part of my career preparation.

## The first step: explaining the result I wanted

I started by describing what I wanted to achieve. GitHub, LinkedIn, and my website are separate platforms, but they served the same purpose for me: presenting myself clearly and consistently.

Here is an example of a prompt for this goal:

> I am preparing for a career in data science. Update my GitHub and LinkedIn profiles using my real experience. Show my work and contact links on my personal website. Create a structure where I can start blogging and easily make updates later. Do not invent experience or achievements.

This is an example that explains my goal, rather than an exact copy of my conversation. A prompt like this describes the task, its purpose, and its limits.

“Make my profile better” can lead to many different results. Explaining my field, my audience, and my wish for an honest presentation gives clearer direction. This experience showed me that writing a good prompt is a way of learning to explain a task clearly.

## What did each tool do?

Different tools supported different parts of the goal. Understanding their roles helps me understand the result.

| Tool or platform | Main role | What it meant in my experience |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ChatGPT | Understand instructions, create text and code, and carry out tasks using available tools | The main workspace where I described what I wanted |
| Browser extension | Provide browser functions, depending on its features and permissions | Part of my setup; its exact role needs to be checked in its documentation |
| GitHub | Store and share project files, code, and a history of changes | A place to make my technical work visible |

I have not included the browser extension's name or technical details here, so I cannot say that the extension alone carried out every task. How ChatGPT completes a task can depend on the connected services, browser tools, and permissions available. What I observed was that I directed the work through ChatGPT without doing each step myself.

## What does “automatic” mean here?

Writing text and changing something in an account are different abilities. An AI model can write a LinkedIn description. To save that description to an account, it also needs a tool that can interact with the platform and the necessary access.

In general, this access can come through a browser tool or a connection provided by an application. An **API** is a way for applications to communicate and perform certain tasks. **Browser automation** can work through the fields and buttons on a webpage. Explaining these methods does not confirm which one was used in my session.

For example, updating a profile description can involve understanding the current information, preparing new text, placing it in the correct field, saving it, and checking the result. An AI system with suitable tools can work through these steps toward a goal. OpenAI's computer use documentation explains how tools can navigate and interact with web interfaces.[1]

In my experience, my role shifted toward explaining the result I wanted. I still have a responsibility to check the information published on my accounts. Automatically written text is not always correct or suitable for me.

## Why do GitHub and LinkedIn matter together?

These platforms have different roles in my career preparation. LinkedIn gives people a quick view of my education and professional interests. GitHub lets people explore the technical details of my projects.

For example, when I share a data analysis project in the future, I can briefly explain the problem on LinkedIn. On GitHub, I can share the code, data preparation steps, and instructions for running the project.

A **README** is useful here. It is an introductory file in a project repository—a folder where the project's files are stored. It can answer questions such as “What does this project do?”, “How do I run it?”, and “What are its limitations?” This gives a recruiter or technical reviewer a starting point for understanding my work.

This is how I see the value of updating my profiles: presenting my real experience clearly. I want to explain my work with specific examples and keep my skills and achievements accurate.

## Why did the website become the center of this process?

My personal website became a place where I could bring information from different platforms together:

**[alperenyrmbs.github.io](https://alperenyrmbs.github.io/)**

I can share my GitHub and LinkedIn links there, explain the ideas behind my projects, and document my learning through blog posts. Sharing this address in a job application can give someone a starting point for learning more about me.

My website address uses the GitHub Pages format. GitHub Pages is a service that publishes HTML, CSS, and JavaScript files from a GitHub repository, sometimes after a build step.[2] It connects the stored website files to the website people visit.

## How can I understand HTML, CSS, and JavaScript?

A blog page is a simple example:

- **HTML** defines the page's content and structure, including headings, paragraphs, and links.
- **CSS** controls how the page looks, including font sizes, colors, spacing, and layout on different screens.
- **JavaScript** adds interaction when needed, such as opening a menu or filtering posts in the browser.

Not every blog feature needs JavaScript. A page with headings, text, and links can work with a simple structure. What matters to me is having a system I can update easily over time.

## Why do I want shared templates and files?

Adding future posts should be as easy as creating the first one. If I recreate the menu, footer, and design for every post, I will have to repeat even small changes across many pages.

A **template** is a shared structure that different pages can use. For example, every post can have the same heading area and layout, while the content changes.

A shared CSS file offers a similar benefit. If I want to increase the font size to make my posts easier to read, I can update one common file instead of changing each page separately.

**Static site generators** can support this approach. They combine content with templates to create the pages that will be published. I am not claiming that a specific generator is installed on my site. My goal is to use a structure that makes writing easier and reduces repeated work.

## What is my first blog post teaching me?

Writing this post is part of the experience. I am explaining what I did and why I did it. Sharing the result this way also helps me understand it better.

I want to use the same approach when writing about future data science projects. For example, if I build a prediction model, I can explain the problem, the data source, the method I chose, and the model's limitations alongside its accuracy. This helps readers evaluate the work.

**Markdown** is another useful concept I came across in this process. It lets me write headings, lists, and links in a simple text file. For example, # Title creates a main heading, and ## Section heading creates a smaller heading. If the website supports it, I can focus on writing while a shared template handles the page design.

## What did I achieve in about 20 minutes?

In my experience, my GitHub and LinkedIn profiles were updated, my personal website was launched, and I started working on my first blog post. The value for my career preparation is that I now have a practical starting point.

This does not yet show that my job applications have become more successful. I do not know the website's visitor numbers, its effect on applications, or its long-term benefits. What I can say is that I have created a more organized space to introduce myself and share my work.

The process also taught me something about how I work: clearly defining a goal, using suitable tools, and reviewing the result all matter. Understanding what AI creates is part of learning to use it well.

## A step toward my career

I see this experience as a step in preparing for my career. I want to grow in data science, and I see explaining my work clearly as part of that preparation.

Over time, I want to develop my website with projects, topics I learn about, and tools I try. Every post does not need to be a major success story. A small experiment, a problem I solved, or a question I am still exploring can also be worth sharing.

This start of about 20 minutes encouraged me to write my first post. My next goal is to keep adding useful content and build a better understanding of the technologies I use. This blog is the first record of that process.
